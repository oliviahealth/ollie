What to Know About the
QUAD SCREENING TEST

What is it?

This test screens the
mother’s blood to look for
four substances: AFP,
Estriol, hCG, and Inhibin-A

AFP (alpha-fetoprotein): A protein produced by the fet A protein produced by the fetus.
Estriol: An estrogen made by both the fetus and the placenta.
hCG: A hormone made in the placenta.

Inhibin-A: A protein made by the placenta and ovaries.

What does it do?

This test screens for certain genetic disorders.
It differs from other screening tests because
it has a higher chance of detecting the risk
of Down syndrome. It does not diagnose a
condition; it only determines whether there is
an increased risk.

How is the test done?

Since this is a blood test, blood is drawn from
the mother and sent to a laboratory for
analysis. The blood draw takes about 10
minutes, and results are typically available

within a few days.

When is it performed?
This test is usually performed between the 16" and 18”
weeks of pregnancy. While it is offered to all women,
some may have a higher risk of having a baby with
genetic disorder. Risk factors include a family history of
birth defects, being 35 years or older, use of certain

medications or substances, and insulin use.
