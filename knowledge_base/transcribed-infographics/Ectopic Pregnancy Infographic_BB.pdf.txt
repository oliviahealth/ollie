ECTOPIC
PREGNANCY

What Is It?

In a typical pregnancy, a fertilized egg
implants in the uterus. In an ectopic
pregnancy, the egg implants outside of
the uterus. Most commonly, this
happens in a fallopian tube, but it can
also occur in the ovary, abdomen, or
cervix. These locations cannot safely
support a growing pregnancy.

Why Is It Dangerous?

The areas outside the uterus are not designed to
support a growing pregnancy. As the pregnancy
grows, it can cause the surrounding tissue to tear,
which may lead to internal bleeding. This can become
serious and requires immediate medical care.

Signs and Symptoms

An ectopic pregnancy can be difficult to recognize
because early symptoms may feel like a normal
pregnancy, including a missed period, nausea, or
fatigue. Warning signs include pelvic or abdominal
pain and vaginal bleeding. Dizziness, fainting, lower
back pain, or feeling lightheaded can also occur and
should be evaluated right away.

What Causes It?

An ectopic pregnancy often occurs when a fallopian
tube is blocked or damaged, preventing the
fertilized egg from reaching the uterus. This can
result from pelvic inflammatory disease (PID),
endometriosis, or scar tissue from previous surgery.

Diagnosis and Treatment

An ultrasound is commonly used to confirm a
suspected ectopic pregnancy. Treatment may
include a medication called methotrexate or a
surgical procedure, depending on the situation. Your
healthcare provider will help determine the safest
and most appropriate option for you.

Contact your provider if you are are pregnant and having
pain, bleeding, or other symptoms of ectopic pregnancy.
