When does it come in?

Breast milk comes in three stages, and
your body starts making it before birth.

1. Colostrum - Day 1
2. Transitional Milk - Day 3 or 4
3. Mature Milk - Between day 10-14

The Three
Breast Milk
Stages

Colostrum

Colostrum is the first milk your body produces and is
present on day one postpartum. It is rich in antibodies
and white blood cells that help protect your baby from
infection. It is usually thick and golden-yellow in color.

Colostrum also helps your baby pass their first stools.
Your body begins producing it during pregnancy, and
your baby will receive colostrum before your milk
transitions to the next stage.

Transitional Milk

Transitional milk usually comes in about 3-4 days after
birth. There is a greater amount of it, and it appears
whiter and thinner than colostrum. It contains more

calories to support your baby’s growth.

Signs that your milk is coming in may include breast
fullness or engorgement, leaking milk, tighter-feeling
skin on the breasts, and an increase in milk supply.

Mature Milk

Mature milk typically comes in around 10-14 days
postpartum. This is the milk your body will
continue to produce for as long as you breastfeed.
It is thinner and often whitish-blue in color, and it
provides all the nutrients your baby needs to grow
and develop.
