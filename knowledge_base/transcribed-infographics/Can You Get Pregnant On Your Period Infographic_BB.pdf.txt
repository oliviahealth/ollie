CAN YOU GET PREGNANT
ON YOUR PERIOD?

Pregnancy is less likely during your period, but it
is still possible. In a typical 28-30 day cycle,
ovulation usually happens several days after your
period ends. However, sperm can live in the body
for up to five days, so if you have a shorter cycle
or ovulate earlier than expected, you could still
become pregnant.

An exception is women with shorter
cycles, around 21-24 days. They
may ovulate soon after their period
ends, and since sperm can live
inside the body for up to five days,
pregnancy is possible if they have
sex during their period.

What about right after your period?

Right after your period ends, you may be entering your
fertile window. Sperm can live in the body for up to five
days, so having sex during this time can lead to
pregnancy—especially if you ovulate earlier than
expected. The closer you get to ovulation, the higher
your chances of conceiving.

What about right before your period?

The chance of pregnancy right before your period is
very low. Ovulation happens earlier in the cycle, and
the egg only lives for 12—24 hours. By the time your
period is about to start, ovulation has already passed,
making this one of the lowest-risk times for
pregnancy—though no time is completely risk-free
without contraception.

Ovulation timing varies for every woman, so
pregnancy chances are different for
everyone. If you are trying to conceive, your
highest chances are in the days leading up to
ovulation. If you are not trying to get
pregnant, earlier cycle days carry lower risk—
but you should still use reliable birth control,
such as condoms or hormonal contraception.
