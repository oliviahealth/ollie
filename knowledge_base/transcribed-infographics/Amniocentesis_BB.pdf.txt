AMNIOCENTESIS

Amniocentesis is a diagnostic test that may be
recommended by your healthcare provider. It is
used to help determine whether certain genetic
conditions or chromosomal abnormalities are
present in your baby.

Amniocentesis can detect chromosomal
abnormalities, neural tube defects, and certain
genetic disorders. Down syndrome is one of the
most common chromosomal abnormalities
identified. Other detectable conditions include
cystic fibrosis and spina bifida. In some cases, the
sample can also be used for DNA analysis, such as
paternity testing.

An ultrasound is used to guide a thin needle
through the abdomen and into the amniotic sac
to collect asmall sample of amniotic fluid. The
entire appointment typically lasts about 45
minutes, but the fluid collection itself usually
takes only a few minutes. The sample is then
sent to a laboratory for analysis, and results are
generally available within a couple of weeks.

Amniocentesis is typically performed between 14
and 20 weeks of pregnancy. In some cases, it may
also be done later in the third trimester, such as
to assess membrane rupture, uterine infections,
lung maturity, or to help manage polyhydramnios.

Amniocentesis is highly accurate, with a success
rate of about 98-99%. It is effective at detecting
genetic and chromosomal abnormalities but
cannot determine the severity of a detected
condition.

Contact your provider for any
questions or concerns!

Serious complications from
amniocentesis are rare. Common side
effects include mild discomfort or
cramping during the procedure.
Afterward, your provider may
recommend resting for the remainder
of the day. Other possible
complications include:

e Miscarriage (less than 1 in 200 women)

e Uterine infection

e Cramping, spotting, or leaking amniotic
fluid

e Rh factor issues

Who Is at Higher Risk of Having
a Baby With a Birth Defect?

Women aged 35 or older

Having had a previous child or pregnancy
with a birth defect

Family history of genetic conditions
Abnormal results on prenatal screening
tests

Testing allows patients to:
e Pursue possible interventions, such as fetal
surgery
e Plan ahead for a child with special needs
e Have time to adjust to upcoming changes
e Access resources early

Some people choose not to test for various
reasons, including:
e Religious or personal beliefs
e Feeling comfortable regardless of the
outcome
e Believing the risks outweigh the benefits

The decision is an individual one, based on
what the parents believe is best.
