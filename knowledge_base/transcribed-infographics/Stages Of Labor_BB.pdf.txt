LABOR

Once you are fully dilated
and effaced, contractions
become very strong, and
you'll feel an intense urge
to push. During this stage,
your baby moves through
the birth canal and is born.
This stage typically Lasts
30 minutes to a few hours.

DELIVERY OF

THE PLACENTA

After your baby and
placenta are delivered, your
body begins to recover. Rest
as much as possible and ask
your provider about ways to

support healing during this
time.

ENJOY YOUR
NEW BABY!

This is when your body begins
preparing for birth. It includes
early, active, and transitional
labor. During this stage, your
cervix begins to dilate and
efface, and contractions
become more frequent and
regular. This is the Longest
stage of labor and can last
anywhere from 2-24 hours,
depending on the person.

PUSHING AND
DELIVERY OF
THE BABY

After your baby is born,
the placenta still needs
to be delivered. Gentle
contractions help your
body release the
placenta. This stage
usually Lasts about 20
minutes.

MATERNAL
PRECOVERY

After all your hard
work, you deserve time
to rest, recover, and
enjoy time with your
baby!
