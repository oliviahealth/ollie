Causes

Mastitis is caused by bacteria
getting trapped in the breast
tissue, resulting in an infection.
Usually, this happens when a
milk duct in the breast gets
clogged, causing milk stasis,
but it can also result from
bacteria entering the breast
through a cracked nipple. Poor
latching, tight bras, and going

too long between nursing can
contribute to mastitis.

Symptoms

Symptoms include swelling, pain, redness, fatigue, and warmth
on the infected breast. Flu-like symptoms often occur.

Treatment

Mastitis is usually treated at home with antibiotics and mild pain

relievers. Continuing to nurse or pump from both breasts helps

relieve blocked ducts. Warm compresses and wearing a loose,
supportive bra may also help.

Prevention

There are several steps you can take to help prevent mastitis.

Ensuring your baby has a proper latch during breastfeeding can

help prevent cracked nipples. Nursing frequently and addressing

clogged ducts as soon as possible can help prevent the buildup
of bacteria.
