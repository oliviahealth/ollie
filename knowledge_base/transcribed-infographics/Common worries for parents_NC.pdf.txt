Common Worries for Parents

Becoming a new parent can bring many worries and fears
about your baby. Here are some of the most common
concerns and helpful information to better understand them.

Worried About Hurting Your
Baby’s Fontanelle

Babies are born with soft spots on
their heads called fontanelles.
Although these areas are delicate
and should be handled gently,
lightly touching or washing them
will not harm your baby.

Worried if your baby will stop
breathing while asleep

Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) is a
common concern for parents. To help
reduce the risk:

e Always place your baby on their back to
sleep.

e Keep the crib clear of blankets, pillows,
toys, crib bumpers, or anything that could
cover your baby’s face.

Worried About Dropping Your Baby
To help prevent falls while holding your baby:
e Check carpets and rugs for areas you
could trip over.
e Keep stairs and doorways clear of clutter.
e Walk slowly and avoid rushing while
carrying your baby.

Worried About Bonding With
Your Baby?

Bonding can take time, and it is common
for new parents to feel unsure at first.
Becoming a parent brings many
emotions. If you feel disconnected from
your baby or notice signs of postpartum
depression, talk to your healthcare
provider for support.

Contact your provider if you have any

questions or concerns.
