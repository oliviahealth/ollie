WHAT IS A PAP SMEAR?

Pap smears screen for cancer and pre-cancerous
changes in the cervix, which is the lower part of
the uterus that opens into the vagina. The test
looks for abnormal cells that could potentially
develop into cervical cancer. Most women aged

21-65 should have a Pap test every few years.

HOW IMPORTANT ARE THEY?

Pap tests can be life-saving. Cervical cancer is

often treatable if detected early. Most women

aged 21-65 should have Pap smears routinely,
even if they are not sexually active or have gone
through menopause. A Pap test is recommended

every three years.

ARE THEY PAINFUL?

Some women find Pap tests uncomfortable,
but they should not be painful. You may feel
pressure when the speculum is inserted.
Using the restroom beforehand and taking
an over-the-counter pain reliever may help

reduce discomfort.

DO I NEED TO PREPARE?

No special preparation is needed before your Pap
test. Avoid douching beforehand, as it is not
recommended at any time. You can still have a Pap
test while on your period, though it is best to schedule
it when you are not menstruating if possible. However,
it is better to have the test during your period than to
miss it altogether.

WHEN WILL I GET MY RESULTS?

You will usually receive your results within 1-3 weeks.

The results may be normal, unclear, or abnormal.

“Unclear” means the doctor cannot determine whether

the cells are normal or abnormal, and you may need
to return for another Pap test. “Abnormal” means
further testing may be needed to determine whether

the cells are precancerous or cancerous.
