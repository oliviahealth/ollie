Understanding Periods
FOR ADOLESCENTS

A period is part of the menstrual cycle and
happens as the body goes through natural
montly changes. Read below to learn more.

WHAT IS IT?

A period happens when blood and tissue leave the uterus and come out of
the vagina. Each month, hormones cause the lining of the uterus to
thicken. This lining is there in case a pregnancy happens. If no pregnancy
occurs that month, the body no longer needs the lining, so it is released
from the body in the form of bleeding. This bleeding is called a period.

WHEN DOES IT HAPPEN?

The age at which a girl gets her first period varies from person to person.
On average, girls tend to get their period between about 10 and 15 years
old, though it can happen earlier or later. Periods may not be regular at
first, but after a couple of years, they usually happen about once a month.
Periods typically last around 5 days, but they can be shorter or longer.

DEALING WITH THE BLOOD

It may look like a lot of blood, but don’t be alarmed. Usually, only a few
tablespoons are lost during the entire period. You can use pads, tampons,
or menstrual cups, and it’s okay to try each one to see which works best
for you.

CONCERNS WITH PERIODS

Contact your provider if you:
e Are 15 and haven’t started your period yet
e Still have irregular periods after about 2 years
e Have periods that last longer than 7 days
e Experience severe cramps or very heavy bleeding

You will have periods until you reach menopause, which happens
around age 45-55. At that time, periods stop permanently. Periods
also stop during pregnancy.

If you have cramps, you can try using a heating pad on your lower
stomach and taking over-the-counter pain reliever like Ibuprofen.
